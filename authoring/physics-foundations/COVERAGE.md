# Physics Foundations Batch 01 — coverage

A focused first expansion, not a complete Physics curriculum. No equal-size target with Mathematics. Existing broad nodes remain, so node counts are not counts of independent competencies.

| Candidate skill | Self-reflection scope |
|---|---|
| Choose and consistently apply a one-dimensional coordinate convention | I can describe the same journey using either axis direction without changing its physical meaning. |
| Calculate average acceleration from two signed velocity readings | I can calculate a signed average acceleration and distinguish it from a claim about every instant. |
| Determine speeding up or slowing down from velocity and acceleration signs | I can classify motion in all four nonzero sign combinations and handle the zero-velocity boundary carefully. |
| Read acceleration from a straight segment of a velocity-time graph | I can calculate a segment’s acceleration without confusing graph height with slope. |
| Recover velocity change from piecewise-constant acceleration areas | I can calculate velocity change from signed rectangular areas and include the initial velocity correctly. |
| Check whether a constant-acceleration model is justified over an interval | I can identify the valid interval and explain what evidence supports the approximation. |
| Calculate final position and velocity over a known constant-acceleration interval | I can advance both position and velocity over a stated valid interval. |
| Carry position and velocity between successive motion intervals | I can connect acceleration, cruising, and braking intervals without losing their initial conditions. |
| Explain velocity and acceleration at the top of an ideal vertical throw | I can distinguish zero instantaneous velocity from zero acceleration at the turning point. |
| Distinguish mass from gravitational weight and calculate weight using a stated g | I can keep mass, gravitational weight, and a scale’s support reading conceptually separate. |
| Identify the direction of a normal contact force | I can draw the normal direction for differently oriented surfaces without assuming its magnitude is mg. |
| Identify the force direction exerted by a taut flexible cable | I can draw the local pulling direction and distinguish a taut cable from a slack one. |
| Check when equal tension may be assumed in an ideal cable model | I can state the assumptions behind equal tension and recognize when they are absent. |
| Infer constant velocity from zero net force in an inertial frame | I can distinguish balanced forces, no forces, constant velocity, and a momentary stop. |
| Audit a free-body diagram for missing and double-counted interactions | I can explain why an extra arrow is invalid and identify an omitted external interaction. |
| Translate a supplied collinear force diagram into a signed Newton equation | I can form and solve the signed equation without inserting an extra ma force. |
| Determine a normal force from a supplied perpendicular force balance | I can find the reaction from the full perpendicular equation rather than automatically setting it equal to weight. |
| Check whether a predicted normal reaction is compatible with nonadhesive contact | I can identify an invalid pulling reaction and explain why the contact model needs revision. |
| Identify which interaction forces cancel when two bodies form one system | I can move between separate-body and combined-system force inventories without discarding external forces. |

## Reuse rather than duplicate

Existing skills cover distance versus displacement, average speed, average velocity, velocity from position graphs, displacement from velocity-time graphs, system boundaries, Newton interaction pairs, force components, static and kinetic friction, and elevator normal-force problems. This batch builds on those existing records. Exact name/ID checks cannot prove semantic uniqueness; Claude must reconcile against the current atlas.
